IBRD FY2024-FY2025 matched-project reporting analysis
Design fixed before numerical trend estimation, 7 October 2026. Not preregistered.
Question: Does allocation reweighting alter the direction/magnitude of changes in disclosed project GHG reporting intensity among continuing projects?
Primary universe: all listed project records, explicit project IDs, active and closed separately tagged. Resolve/exclude ambiguous parent/additional-financing ID pairs. Missing GHG is not zero. Repayment removal is not physical disappearance.
Primary matched sample: numeric annual GHG in both reports, positive eligible commitment and positive allocation at both endpoints, same reporting-status category (active/active or closed/closed). Transition projects reported separately. Full valid matched sample is a sensitivity, not a substitute for the primary sample.
Primary construct: allocation-weighted whole-project annual reported GHG divided by green-bond eligible committed USD million. This is a reporting diagnostic, not financed/attributed physical impact or an EIB-equivalent project-cost intensity. Direct project cost unavailable; do not reconstruct it mechanically from rounded shares and commitments with mixed valuation dates.
Replicate exact symmetric within-project and reweighting decomposition; within-project GHG and inverse-commitment components using the same Shapley principle. Cost and eligibility are not separately identified from eligible commitment. Secondary reported-IBRD-share-adjusted numerator diagnostic, explicitly analyst constructed.
Prespecified checks: fixed FY24 weights; fixed FY25 weights; symmetric weights; frozen FY24 commitments to isolate GHG updates; active and closed strata; renewable/efficiency subset; leave-one-project and leave-one-country out; financing rounding sensitivity matching FY24 precision; sample allocation/GHG coverage; numeric entrants/exits and source reconciliation.
No p-values or causal/realized-calendar-year claims. IBRD fiscal-year report vintages may include later ICR availability. Currency conversion and cancellations may affect commitments. Strong inference requires source notes and independent extraction verification.
