RESULTS AND METHODS FOR MANUSCRIPT USE
Analysis date: 7 October 2026. Read together with README.txt and the independent reviews.

Core empirical interpretation
The annual allocation-weighted reporting comparison changes materially under a common denominator rule, yet the matched-project expected-intensity comparison becomes small once allocation weights and the known financial-stream boundary are held explicit. These answer different questions. Annual allocation composition genuinely matters to the reporting statistic; removing it does not prove that the annual-allocation statistic is erroneous. What the comparison cannot establish is broad revision of projects' expected mitigation or realized deterioration in carbon outcomes.

Population and coverage
The source census contains 461 CAB project-year rows: 138 in 2022, 158 in 2023 and 165 in 2024. Numeric absolute/relative pairs number 93, 116 and 109. The separate 2024 EuGBS stream adds 14 rows, all numeric. There is one 2024 dual-stream project, so the combined year contains 178 unique IDs. Twelve raw relative-GHG zero payloads concealed under merged below-threshold status cells are missing disclosures, not numeric zeros.

2023 CAB allocations total EUR15,837.996039m, of which EUR11,419.463026m attaches to numeric GHG (72.101691%). In 2024, EUR16,654.931211m total and EUR10,853.439599m numeric imply coverage 65.166523%. Unavailable-GHG finance consequently grows from EUR4,418.533013m to EUR5,801.491612m, while coverage falls 6.935168 percentage points. The 2024 missing allocation comprises EUR3,441.549060m deferred framework/intermediated reporting, EUR1,350.110150m below-threshold reporting, and EUR1,009.832402m withheld after the stated external-review concerns. These mechanisms cannot be pooled into a physical zero or a common censoring interval.

Three annual comparisons
Reported relative-GHG intensity: −228.838680 in 2023 and −195.777201 in 2024, 14.447505% lower magnitude. Both summaries are hardcoded numeric cells. The computational basis reproducing 2023 uses all allocations; the basis reproducing 2024 uses allocations to numeric-GHG rows.
Common observed-allocation rule: −317.383234 to −195.777201, 38.315204% lower magnitude.
Common all-allocation disclosed-numerator rule: −228.838680 to −127.581195, 44.248413% lower magnitude.
Units are expected annual tCO2e per EUR million eligible project cost, allocation-weighted. The difference between 14.45% and 38.32% is an economically meaningful interpretation wedge in the same annual reports, but neither series measures realized impact or identifies how investors react.

Fixed population, scope and weights
Of 64 continuing CAB IDs, 48 have numeric GHG in both years. This subset has EUR4,557.056220m and EUR4,340.759888m annual allocations, 28.773% and 26.063% of complete CAB allocations. It is selected and cannot stand in for the full portfolio.
On whole-project cost, the 48-project annual-weight statistic changes −431.667378 to −249.604427, a 42.176676% magnitude decline. Holding normalized 2023 weights fixed instead yields −431.667378 to −430.602326, a 0.246730% magnitude decline. Holding 2024 weights gives a 1.141791% decline; the symmetric reference gives 0.577052%. These deterministic alternatives are not confidence bounds.

After adding 2024 EuGBS and deduplicating whole-project records, 55 IDs have numeric GHG in both the 2023 CAB population and the combined 2024 boundary. Their allocations are EUR5,709.262096m and EUR5,625.059140m, 36.048% of 2023 CAB allocations and 28.907% of combined 2024 allocations. Whole-project-cost annual-weight intensity changes −352.533977 to −200.547067: −43.112698% in magnitude. The corresponding magnitude changes are −0.241143% at fixed 2023 weights, −1.097128% at fixed 2024 weights, and −0.553709% with symmetric normalized reference weights. If cost and weights are both frozen at 2023 values, GHG revisions alone raise the magnitude 0.051928%. This is the strongest boundary-aware residual comparison. It is still an ex-post selected-cohort diagnostic, not complete historical stream harmonization.

The exact 55-project whole-cost symmetric decomposition allocates the signed +151.986910 change as +150.449522 allocation reweighting, +1.738148 project-cost updates and −0.200760 GHG revisions. Thus reweighting accounts for 98.988473% of this particular accounting change. This percentage is an accounting share, not a causal proportion. Whole-project GHG entries change materially in only three of the 48 CAB numeric continuers; one is consistent with increased displayed precision.

The eligible-cost fixed-2023-weight comparison among the 48 CAB continuers instead shows a 5.318890% magnitude rise. This is not the main performance finding: it is dominated by Normandie's CAB eligibility falling 100% to 2% as 98% is assigned to EuGBS, while a hypothetical old allocation weight is retained. Excluding Normandie changes the fixed-weight magnitude comparison to −0.246384%, consistent with the whole-cost control. This is a concrete illustration of why constructed denominator factors and allocation weights should not be read as independently manipulable physical drivers.

Three-year selected cohort
Only 23 IDs disclose numeric GHG in all three CAB reports. Annual-weight eligible-cost values are −487.580985, −655.678171 and −493.361951. At fixed 2022 weights, cost and eligibility they are −487.580985, −487.782395 and −488.169962. This illustrates report-weight variation under nearly stable disclosed GHG for that subset; the 2022 source definition changes still prevent treating it as an unrestricted realized-outcome panel.

Entry/exit and exact arithmetic
Let O_t be the numeric-GHG set, D_t=sum over O_t of A_it, w_it=A_it/D_t and s_it=1000G_it/(C_it e_it). I_obs,t=sum over O_t of w_it s_it. For common numeric records, delta(w*s)=mean(w)*delta(s)+mean(s)*delta(w). Add current contributions of observed-set additions and subtract previous contributions of removals. This is exact and requires no unobserved GHG counterfactuals. In the 2023–24 full CAB numeric-set decomposition, 48 continuers contribute +72.823459, 61 numeric report entrants contribute −94.807184, 65 numeric report exits contribute +143.501783 and three numeric-to-missing transitions contribute +0.087975. There are no missing-to-numeric transitions among table continuers. Total +121.606034 equals the observed-allocation change. Report exit means absence from the annual allocation sheet, not physical cessation.

For the intensity component of numeric continuers, write s=x1*x2*x3 with x1=1000G, x2=1/C and x3=1/e. For each factor, average its marginal contribution over all six possible update orders. The sum is exactly delta(s); factors with no change contribute zero. Whole-cost sensitivity sets e=1. This is standard Shapley/order-averaged accounting, not a new method or identification strategy. Costs and shares must be positive and finite and allocations nonnegative; all actual source allocations are strictly positive. Signed GHG may be positive, zero or negative. The source fields approved EIB share and signed loan are not used in the primary formula.

Coverage and reconstructed rule change
Let c_t=D_t/T_t, with T_t total allocation. I_all,t=c_t I_obs,t. The exact symmetric decomposition of delta(I_all) is mean(c)*delta(I_obs)+mean(I_obs)*delta(c). For 2023–24 these are +83.463216 and +17.794270, totaling +101.257485.
A published-minus-observed trend wedge is benchmark dependent; it is not itself a pure convention effect. The 2023–24 published-minus-observed wedge is −88.544554; published-minus-all wedge is −68.196006.
A supplementary three-input symmetric bridge evaluates f(I,c,z)=I[c+(1−c)z], with z=0 for the computational all-allocation basis and z=1 for the observed-allocation basis. These z values are numerical reconstructions, not observed preparer intentions. Averaging all six update orders gives +103.237423 conditional-intensity contribution, +9.599933 actual-coverage contribution and −79.775877 reconstructed-rule contribution, totaling the published +33.061479 change. For 2022–23 relative summaries the reconstructed-rule contribution is exactly zero. Do not stack this bridge with unscaled lower-level I_obs components; the levels overlap.

Sector structure, concentration and deterministic sensitivity
Main eligible-activity grouping accounts for most of the observed-portfolio movement within the electricity/energy category (+92.005625 signed contribution change) and manufacturing (+30.824432), partly offset by transport/storage (−4.465235). These are categories of the main eligible activity, not proof of a common whole-project GHG sector boundary. In particular, manufacturing GHG boundary exclusions inhibit universal sector performance rankings.
The top five absolute signed contributions represent 69.146708% in 2023 and 65.626266% in 2024. Tyrrhenian Link is the largest contributor; its disclosed relative GHG remains −6,316 kt/year and cost EUR3,800m, while its allocation changes EUR900m to EUR500m. Concentration does not make the observed annual trend a one-project artifact: removing any single ID from both years leaves a magnitude decline between 31.475772% and 44.598595%. Removing the union of each year's top five contributions leaves a 40.606248% decline. These are deterministic deletion sensitivities, not sampling uncertainty.
Observed whole-cost CAB intensity falls 37.424918% in magnitude; annual e=100% records fall 36.427824%; renewable-generation activities fall 27.899441%; excluding manufacturing gives 32.849658%; investment loans give 38.042295%. Populations differ; these are explicit alternative descriptive questions.
Adding EuGBS and deduplicating to 178 projects gives combined 2024 whole-cost observed intensity −158.538671 versus 2023 CAB −306.645393: 48.299021% lower magnitude. The comparator CAB-only whole-cost decline is 37.424918%. This boundary sensitivity does not erase the annual-weight trend; the fixed-cohort tests show that the broad direction cannot be reinterpreted as broad GHG-expectation revision. The shared project's relative figure is identical across streams; choosing 15.2 versus 15.0 absolute kt/year changes combined absolute intensity only 27.471411 to 27.469900.

Missing-GHG scenarios
A stipulated portfolio-total statistic would equal I_all,t+(1−c_t)m_t, where m_t is the assumed allocation-weighted intensity of missing records. No estimate of m_t is supplied by these workbooks. The grid varies m_2023 and m_2024 explicitly. If the same missing-record intensity is imposed in both years, the two scenario totals tie at m=−1460.058091 tCO2e/year per EURm. This is algebra under a stated assumption, not a physical threshold, identified bound, probability statement or evidence that such an average is plausible. Missing mechanisms and project scopes differ, so no universal censoring interval is asserted.

Mandatory claim boundaries
Three final annual editions from one issuer are not a causal or generalizable cross-market panel. Appraisal expectations, cost revisions, method vintages, missingness and final retrospective financial boundaries coexist. The original workbook summaries are hardcoded, and exact arithmetic reconstruction cannot prove the preparer's intended formula. The 2022 absolute residual remains unresolved. Known 2024 stream reconciliation can be handled, but broader historical financial boundary equality cannot be assumed. These constraints limit claims to reproducible disclosure comparability and what an analyst may infer from report-to-report changes.
